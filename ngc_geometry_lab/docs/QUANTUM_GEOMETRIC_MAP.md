## 6. Observable Lie / Jordan Algebra & Geometric Uncertainty

Quantum mechanics traditionally combines two distinct algebraic structures into an associative complex matrix product $A B$:
\[
A B = \frac{1}{2}(A B + B A) + \frac{1}{2}(A B - B A) = A \circ B + \frac{i}{2} [A, B],
\]
where $A \circ B$ is the **Jordan product** (symmetric, commutative, real/Hermitian) and $[A, B]$ is the **Lie bracket** (anti-symmetric, anti-commutative, anti-Hermitian).

### 6.1 Geometric Decomposition
For any two single-qubit observables $A = a_0 I_2 + a \cdot \sigma$ and $B = b_0 I_2 + b \cdot \sigma$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{R}^3$:
1. **Jordan Product (Symmetric State Update / Compatibility):**
   \[
   \boxed{A \circ B = (a_0 b_0 + a \cdot b) I_2 + (a_0 b + b_0 a) \cdot \sigma}.
   \]
   For traceless observables ($a_0 = b_0 = 0$), $A \circ B = (a \cdot b) I_2$. The Jordan product directly extracts the Euclidean scalar product!
2. **Lie Bracket (Precession Generator / Oriented Area):**
   \[
   \boxed{[A, B] = 2i (a \times b) \cdot \sigma}.
   \]
   The commutator is isomorphic to the **vector cross product $a \times b$**, which represents the oriented bivector plane of rotation.

### 6.2 The Geometric Robertson Oriented-Volume Uncertainty Bound
In state $\rho = \frac{1}{2}(I_2 + r \cdot \sigma)$ with Bloch vector $\|r\| \le 1$:
\[
\frac{1}{2i} \langle [A, B] \rangle_\rho = (a \times b) \cdot r = \det([a, b, r]).
\]
The Robertson-Schrödinger uncertainty relation takes the direct spatial form:
\[
\boxed{\Delta_\rho(A) \Delta_\rho(B) \ge |(a \times b) \cdot r| = |\det([a, b, r])|}.
\]
**Geometric Meaning & Lower-Bound Distinction:**
- Quantum measurement uncertainty is **bounded below by the oriented volume of the parallelepiped** spanned by measurement axes $a, b$ and state vector $r$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$.
- **Lower Bound $\ne$ Actual Variance Product:** On general mixed states or non-eigenstates (e.g. $r = (0.5, 0.5, 0)^T$ for $X, Y$), the volume lower bound is $0.0$, while the actual product of standard deviations is $\Delta X \Delta Y = \sqrt{0.75} \sqrt{0.75} = 0.75 > 0$.
- **Exact Saturation:** Saturation ($\Delta A \Delta B = |\det[a, b, r]|= 1.0$) occurs on pure eigenstates of the commutator axis $(a \times b)$ (e.g., pure state $|0\rangle$ on the $z$-axis with $X$ and $Y$ measurements).
