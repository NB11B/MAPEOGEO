# GEO-PRIME-001 — Relational states and operator sequences

## Hypothesis
Prime recognition is a property of the relation state R after generating numbers and closing multiplicative factorizations, not a property of positional-base notation. Operator scheduling changes intermediate knowledge, but **not** mathematical primality in the completed integer structure.

## Experiment
1. Generate positive integers from 2 to 1000. The odd candidates are also generated by n+(n+1), n>=1, with 2 handled separately.
2. For each new integer, record nontrivial factor pairs only if both factor vertices are already available. This models an event-sensitive update of R.
3. Compare ascending against descending one-pass updates. A descending pass cannot yet see smaller factors and produces 831 *false apparent primes* out of the 831 composites between 2 and 1000.
4. Apply complete factor closure independent of ordering.
5. Score against an independently executed trial-division oracle.

## Results
Initial local independent prototype 5/5 tests pass. Reproducible committed suite has 7 tests and must be qualified by CI separately. At limit 1000, ascending and completed closure both yield 168 primes, zero errors; descending one-pass erroneously leaves 831 composites without factor witnesses.

## Distinctions
- This is an epistemic/order-of-discovery effect, **not** physical dynamical changes in primality.
- A lack of factor edges is not a primality certificate until search is complete.
- Factor enumeration uses arithmetic division/modulo explicitly. Therefore the mechanism **does not autonomously discover multiplication** from successive addition.
- A native MAPEOGEO graph representation and composition-grammar discovery remain future gates.

## Next falsification gates
GP2: treat successor, addition, and repeated addition/multiplication as independently learned transition compositions.
GP3: operator scheduling and closure confluence versus interim noncommutativity.
GP4: identify composite witness certificates and prime *absence* certificates with bounded divisor-exhaustion proof.
GP5: infer prime gap structure without supplying factor-test features and compare against sieve/trial division.
GP6: PSMSL native relational graph integration, blind holdouts, and mathematical proof audit.
